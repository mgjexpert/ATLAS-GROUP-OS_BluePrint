# 00 — Vision

## The objective

Build an agentic enterprise ecosystem in which the founder acts primarily as **PDG / Chairman**, defining strategic direction, capital allocation and material approvals, while a governed network of AI agents performs a growing portion of operational, technical, administrative, research, content, commercial and management work.

The target is not unrestricted autonomous AI. The target is **high capability under explicit governance**.

## Product layers

### ATLAS GROUP OS
The enterprise operating layer.

It understands:
- legal/operational organizations;
- business units and branches;
- clients and contracts;
- products and services;
- projects and portfolios;
- budgets and cost centers;
- human and agent workforce;
- tools and infrastructure;
- goals, decisions and approvals;
- risks, incidents and audit records.

### ATLAS
The human-facing assistant.

ATLAS becomes the interface through which the PDG asks:
- What needs my attention?
- Which projects are off track?
- What decisions are waiting?
- What is costing too much?
- What should be paused, accelerated or reviewed?
- Open this project on my computer.
- Prepare the Board briefing.

### ATLAS DG
The Group General Director / operational executive agent.

It consumes Group state, plans, delegates, follows up and reports. It does not need to be a coding agent.

### ATLAS.DEV
The technology director / engineering organization.

It receives objectives from ATLAS DG or the PDG and uses engineering agent systems to plan, build, test, review and deploy under policy.

## Enterprise model

The Atlas Group may operate three types of entities:

1. **Internal shared-services units** — Dev, Content, Growth, CX, Research, Admin, Finance Ops.
2. **External managed-service branches** — dedicated operations for clients.
3. **Owned ventures** — products and businesses created, tested and scaled by Atlas.

A branch can be logical (a tenant inside shared infrastructure) or physical (a dedicated VPS/stack connected to Atlas HQ through a control plane).

## Human role

Humans remain accountable for:
- company/legal representation;
- material contracts;
- bank and payment authority;
- regulated decisions;
- hiring/firing decisions with legal consequences;
- major capital allocation;
- irreversible/destructive actions;
- governance changes.

Agents may prepare, recommend and execute within delegated mandates.

## Core design principle

**Capability is not authority.**

An agent may technically be capable of performing an action while still being prohibited from doing it without approval.

## Long-term outcome

A Group in which the PDG speaks mainly to ATLAS, while ATLAS coordinates:
- digital executives;
- departments;
- specialized agent teams;
- human staff;
- client operations;
- ventures;
- infrastructure;
- external tools.

The measurable goal is increased operational leverage per human decision, not agent count.
